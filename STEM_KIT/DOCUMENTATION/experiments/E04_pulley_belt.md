# Experiment E04 — Belts and pulleys: speed, force and direction

**Kit:** Pulley lab (Kit 8) · **Levels:** Explorer (count turns), Engineer (ratio, tension), Inventor (slip & efficiency)

## Question
How does pulley size change how fast and how strongly the output turns? What
does a crossed belt do?

## Prediction
Motor pulley Ø20, output pulley Ø60. For 30 turns of the motor pulley, the
output turns ____ times, in the ☐ same ☐ opposite direction.

## Build
Assembly guide 04. Put tape flags on both pulleys. Slide the belt mounts
along their slots until the O-ring belt is just tight (it should deflect about
3 mm when pressed with one finger), then lock the thumb nuts.

## Experiment
1. Turn the motor pulley by hand 30 turns (motor unplugged), count output turns.
2. Swap to Ø40 → Ø40, then Ø60 → Ø20.
3. Cross the belt (figure-8). Watch the direction.
4. Tension: loosen the belt 5 mm, hang a small weight (bag of coins) on a
   string wound on the output shaft and power the motor. When does it slip?

## Measure

| Driver Ø | Driven Ø | Driver turns | Driven turns | Measured ratio | Predicted ratio (Ø2 / Ø1) | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 60 | 30 | | | 3 | |
| 40 | 40 | 30 | | | 1 | |
| 60 | 20 | 10 | | | 1/3 | |
| 20 (crossed) | 60 | 30 | | | 3 | |

## Explain
The belt moves at one speed, so a small pulley must turn more times than a
big one: ratio = D_driven / D_driver. A 3:1 reduction gives ⅓ the speed and
(ideally) 3× the torque. A crossed belt reverses direction. Belts slip when
the load torque beats the friction between belt and groove — tension
increases friction; that is why the mounts are slotted.

## Build it wrong on purpose
Mount the two pulleys out of line by 5 mm (move one along its shaft). Run it.
Why does the belt climb off?

## Challenge
Make the output turn at exactly ⅓ of the motor speed but in the opposite
direction, using only the parts in the lab. Then: make it turn 9× slower
(hint: combine pulleys and gears with the coupling holes).
