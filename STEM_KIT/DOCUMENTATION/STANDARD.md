# The ForgeGrid mechanical standard

Every STEM_KIT part follows the rules below. All of them are variables in
[`PARAMETERS/config.scad`](../PARAMETERS/config.scad); change a value there,
run `python3 tools/build.py`, and the whole library regenerates.

## 1. The grid

| Rule | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hole pitch | **10 mm** | Big enough for children's fingers, small enough for fine adjustment |
| Hole diameter | **3.4 mm** (M3 clearance) | Same hole takes an M3 screw *or* a 3 mm shaft (plain bushing) |
| Edge offset | **5 mm** from every plate edge | Plates placed edge to edge keep one continuous lattice |
| Plate / rail thickness | **6 mm** | Stiff; room for a 3 mm deep hex nut pocket under every hole |
| Foot / flange thickness | **4 mm** | 4 mm foot + 6 mm plate = **M3 × 10**, the kit's standard screw |

A part is "on the grid" when its holes land on lattice points. Plates, rails,
chassis, tower flanges, link bars, frame plates and the solar-panel frame back
all carry the lattice, so any mount bolts to any of them.

## 2. Fasteners — M3 only

| Joint | Hardware |
|---|---|
| Mount foot (4 mm) → plate or rail (6 mm) | M3 × 10 button head, nut in the plate's hex pocket |
| Plate/rail (6) → plate/rail (6), tower flange stacking | M3 × 12 |
| Tilt pivot, pillar ends | M3 × 10 into a captive nut |
| Hub set screws | M3 × 6 grub screw + M3 nut in the hub's nut slot |
| Crank / linkage knob | M3 × 40 + nut + nylock |
| Linkage pivots | M3 × 12 / × 16 + nut + nylock |

Button heads (ISO 7380) are the standard: low (1.65 mm), rounded, child safe.
Nuts are always captive (hex pockets or side-entry slots) or hand-tightened
thumb nuts, so children never need a spanner. One 2 mm hex key does everything.

## 3. Shafts and axis heights

* **Shaft: Ø3 mm steel rod** (cut from 3 × 330 mm silver-steel / stainless rod).
  Printed Ø3 shafts exist for light duty. `shaft_diameter = 4` or `5` rebuilds
  the whole system for 4/5 mm shafts.
* Bores: **3.15 mm** (hub, locked with a set screw) and **3.4 mm**
  (free-running bushing / idler).
* **Horizontal shafts sit 17 mm above the surface they are bolted to**
  (`axis_height`). Every bearing mount, axle mount and motor mount obeys it, so
  any two of them bolted to the same surface are coaxial or parallel.
  The high standard is **37 mm** (`axis_height_high`, = +2 pitches) for big
  pulleys.
* In a **gearbox frame** (two lattice plates 48 mm apart), shafts run straight
  through the grid holes — every lattice point is a bushing.

## 4. Gears: "any gear meshes any gear"

* Module **2**, 20° pressure angle, 6 mm wide, tooth counts **10, 20, 30, 40**.
* With module 2, centre distance = (z1 + z2) mm. Tooth counts that are
  multiples of 10 make **every** centre distance a multiple of 10 mm — so any
  two gears mesh when their shafts are in grid holes. 3-4-5 diagonals also
  work (a 40T + 10T pair meshes across a 30 × 40 diagonal = 50 mm).
* Gears are generated by a simulated rack cutter (like a real hobbing
  machine), so 10-tooth pinions get the correct undercut; 0.30 mm total
  backlash per mesh; verified: constant 0.134–0.138 mm flank gap through a
  full tooth cycle, zero overlap, and the pair locks if backlash is removed.
* Every gear ≥ 20T has coupling holes on the 10 mm radius → bolt a gear to a
  wheel, pulley or another gear to make a compound.
* Gear levels in the gearbox frame (y measured from plate A's outer face):
  **A** teeth 14–20, **M** teeth 27.5–33.5, **B** teeth 38–44. Hubs face the
  nearer plate.

## 5. Common hub

Ø14 × 7 mm hub, radial M3 set screw at mid-height, nut slot open to the hub
top. The same hub is on every gear, wheel, pulley, the shaft collar, the motor
coupler and the turbine hub: once a child can fix one, they can fix all.

## 6. Motors, bearings, panels (bought parts)

* Motor: **130-size DC motor** (Ø20.2, 15.2 across flats, 25.2 long, 2 mm
  shaft). All motor-holding parts derive from `motor_size`.
* Bearing housing: **625** (5 × 16 × 5). The 623 adapter ring (for 3 mm
  shafts) or a printed plain bushing drop into the same pocket.
* Solar panel: **110 × 69 × 3 mm** epoxy panel (5 V ~1 W). The frame is
  generated from `solar_panel_size`, so other panels only need a rebuild.

## 7. Printing defaults

0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, PLA or PETG, 2.4 mm preferred walls (1.6 mm
minimum), 0.2 mm sliding clearance per side (`tolerance`; calibrate with the
tolerance test). Horizontal holes are teardrops; pockets are truncated
teardrops; lips and flanks are 45°; nothing needs support material.

## 8. Known consequences of the standard

* On a flat plate, a horizontal shaft at 17 mm can carry gears up to 10T
  (Ø24) only; bigger gears must use a gearbox frame, a slotted chassis, the
  37 mm high mounts, or overhang a plate edge. This is deliberate: the frame
  is where gear trains live, and it keeps fingers away from big gears.
* Bearing mounts are 22 mm wide, so two of them side by side need ≥ 30 mm
  between shafts (use axle mounts, 12 mm wide, for 20 mm centre distances).
